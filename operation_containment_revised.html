OPERATION CONTAINMENT — NSC PLAYER FILE
1
THE SITUATION
It is 1947. The Soviet Union is aggressively expanding its sphere of influence. President Truman has appointed you Senior Policy Advisor to the National Security Council — the most powerful foreign policy body in America.
Three regions are at immediate risk: Korea (a divided peninsula where Soviet-backed North Korean forces are massing), Iran (a democratic government sitting atop massive oil reserves coveted by both superpowers), and Guatemala (a popular elected government whose land reforms are alarming US corporate and intelligence interests).
You have 12 turns — representing 1947 to 1963 — to manage these crises. Each turn, you allocate your budget across the three regions using one of six possible actions. At the end, you are scored on two things simultaneously: how much Soviet influence you contained, AND how democratic your allied governments still are. The gap between those two scores is the point of the game.
🎯 The core tension: The most effective ways to stop Soviet influence are also the most harmful to the democratic values America claims to defend. You will feel this tension every turn.
2
YOUR FOUR METERS — WHAT THEY MEAN
🔴 SOVIET INFLUENCE
Measures communist penetration across your three theatres combined. You want this LOW. If you take no action in a region, it rises by 4% per turn as Soviet pressure mounts.
🟢 DEMOCRACY INDEX
Measures how free and democratic your allied governments remain. You want this HIGH. Covert operations, military pressure, and propaganda all erode it. This is the second half of your final score.
🟡 FEAR LEVEL
Rises automatically when historical crisis events occur (Soviet nuclear test, China falling to Mao, etc.). This is not just a warning — it unlocks new actions. Without sufficient Fear, you cannot access propaganda, coups, or proxy funding. You cannot manually raise Fear; you must wait for events.
🔵 BLOWBACK RISK
Every covert action raises this. If it hits 100%, you lose immediately. This represents the real historical phenomenon — CIA coups and proxy wars created anti-American sentiment that eventually exploded (Iran 1979, Guatemala civil war 1960–1996). Once you start using covert tools, blowback accumulates fast.
3
HOW EACH TURN WORKS — STEP BY STEP
STEP 1 — Crisis Event (automatic): At the start of certain turns, a red CRISIS BULLETIN popup appears. This represents a real historical event — the Soviet nuclear test, China falling to communism, etc. Read it, then click Acknowledge. It raises your Fear level, which may unlock new actions.
STEP 2 — Select a Region: Click one of the three glowing circles on the world map. Iran is in the Middle East, Korea is on the Korean peninsula in East Asia, and Guatemala is in Central America. Each region has its own independent Soviet and Democracy meters.
STEP 3 — Read the Intelligence Brief: Below the map, you'll see a brief describing what is actually happening in that region at this point in history. This is not decorative — it gives you the context you need to understand why your decision is so difficult.
STEP 4 — Choose an Action: The right panel shows available actions for the selected region. Each has a dollar cost and, for some, a minimum Fear level. You can afford multiple actions per turn if your budget allows — you can even switch regions between actions.
STEP 5 — Read the Consequence Report: After every action, a popup appears showing exactly what changed: Soviet Influence (should go down), Democracy (may go down), Blowback Risk (may go up), and Budget Spent. It also shows real historical context for that type of action. Do not skip this — it is the educational core of the game.
STEP 6 — End the Turn: Click END TURN when done. The year advances, budgets reset to $8M, and any regions you ignored will see Soviet influence rise automatically. Turns 9–12 give you $11M per turn as Phase III resources expand.
💡 You can act in multiple regions per turn. After taking action in Korea, you can click Iran or Guatemala and act there too — as long as you have budget remaining. Plan your spending before ending the turn.
4
THE SIX ACTIONS — COMPLETE BREAKDOWN
ACTIONCOSTREQUIRESEFFECTS & HISTORICAL NOTE
DIPLOMATIC AID $2M Nothing — always available Soviet −6% · Democracy +5% · Blowback: none.
Modeled on the Marshall Plan (1948). Slow but sustainable. The only action that actually improves democracy.
MILITARY ADVISORS $3M Nothing — always available Soviet −12% · Democracy −7% · Blowback +8%.
Governments become dependent on US military support and grow authoritarian. Used extensively in Korea, Iran, and Latin America throughout the 1950s.
PROPAGANDA $2M Fear must be 30%+ Soviet −9% · Democracy −4% · Blowback +5%.
CIA ran covert disinformation in Iran (1953) and Guatemala — portraying elected leaders as Soviet agents. Effective, but destroys free press and public trust.
CIA COVERT COUP $4M Fear must be 50%+ Soviet −25% · Democracy −30% · Blowback +22%.
Based on Operation AJAX (Iran, 1953) and PBSUCCESS (Guatemala, 1954). Highly effective short-term. Both created catastrophes within a generation: Iran 1979, Guatemalan civil war through 1996.
INSTALL LEADER $5M Fear must be 60%+ Soviet −30% · Democracy −38% · Blowback +26%.
Names the actual US-backed ruler: Shah Pahlavi, Syngman Rhee, or Castillo Armas. This is the clearest version of the game's argument: containment starts as fear, then becomes control over who governs.
PROXY MILITIA FUNDING $3M Fear must be 65%+ Soviet −16% · Democracy −22% · Blowback +16%.
Arming paramilitaries means losing control of what they do. CIA-funded proxy forces in Guatemala and later across Latin America committed widespread atrocities. This playbook repeated through the 1980s with similar results.
⚠ Locked actions appear grayed out. They are locked because either (a) your Fear level is too low — wait for the next Crisis Event — or (b) you don't have enough budget this turn. You cannot unlock Fear manually; it only rises through historical events.
5
WINNING, LOSING & YOUR FINAL SCORE
LOSE CONDITION: If your Blowback Risk meter hits 100%, you lose immediately. The populations you over-intervened in have turned against American interests — exactly as Iran did in 1979 after 26 years of CIA-backed authoritarian rule.
END CONDITION: After Turn 12, the game ends regardless of your meters. You receive your final scores.
YOUR FINAL SCORE IS TWO NUMBERS:

🟢 Soviet Influence Contained = 100% minus the average Soviet influence remaining across all three regions. High is good.

🔴 Democracy Preserved = Average democracy index remaining across all three regions. High is good.

The gap between these two numbers is the entire argument of the game. A score of 85% Contained / 15% Democracy means you "won" the Cold War by building an empire of authoritarian client states. A score of 40% Contained / 72% Democracy means you kept your principles but Soviet influence ran high. Almost no one achieves both above 60% simultaneously — that tension is intentional, and it mirrors the real historical dilemma that Truman and Eisenhower actually faced.
6
THE MAP — NAVIGATION
Use the + and − buttons above the map to zoom in and out. Click and drag to pan around. Click ⌂ to reset to the default view.
The glowing, pulsing circles show where your three intervention zones are. Their color reflects Soviet influence: blue = low (good), orange = contested, red = high (danger). They grow larger as Soviet influence increases — a visual heat map of communist penetration.
The larger red and amber shadows show communist bloc power and contested countries outside your direct control, including the USSR, Eastern Europe, China, Cuba, Vietnam, Egypt, and Indonesia. Dashed arrows show pressure moving from the communist bloc toward crisis regions as fear rises.
Click directly on the circle or the region label to select that region.
